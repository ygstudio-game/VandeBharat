Standalone architecture reference

Framing, Preprocessing & Synchronization

A clean-slate design for the three layers underneath the model pipeline — written to stand on its own, elaborated in full, and corrected against how coach assignment actually works: gap detection on the OCR camera, not a generic presence signal.

Scope Capture policy · preprocessing tasks · coach-boundary assignment · dashboard hierarchy Input 7 genlocked cams, mp4 per pass Updated 2026-07-11

00Overview

One decision drives everything below: input arrives as genlocked mp4 files, one complete recording per camera per train pass — not a live per-frame socket. That makes synchronization a batch manifest-building problem: build one correct, complete record of what happened, once, before any model runs — not a live-stream timeout/join problem where you're guessing whether a message is still coming.

The other load-bearing fact, corrected in this version: coach boundaries aren't inferred from a vague "presence signal." The OCR camera itself detects the physical gap between two coaches and timestamps it. Everything captured between two consecutive gap timestamps — from any of the 7 cameras — belongs to whichever coach number the OCR camera read in that same window. That's the actual assignment rule, and it's simpler and more auditable than anything inferred statistically.

01Framing

Three extraction policies, one per camera role, all reconciled against the shared genlock clock. Framing isn't "pick a frame rate" here — each role has a different physical reason for its own policy, and a dropped frame is accounted for explicitly rather than assumed away.

All 3 lanes share the same shape — integrity check, parse genlock index, role-specific extraction, stamp envelope, write ledger. They differ only in the amber policy box.

Walkthrough

  1. Files land per pass. Each of the 7 cameras writes its own mp4 for a train pass. Nothing starts until all 7 are present and readable.
  2. Integrity check first, always. A truncated or corrupt file (power blip, storage hiccup — normal in a rail yard, not exceptional) gets quarantined and alerted immediately, not discovered three stages later when a model chokes on it.
  3. Genlock index is the join key. Every frame's physical index and embedded timestamp is read from the container before anything else happens — this is what every later synchronization step keys off, not wall-clock time.
  4. Extraction policy differs by physical role, not preference. OCR only needs the plate read once — trimming empty roll saves calls. Component cameras must sample at an identical stride or the 4-way tile desyncs geometrically. Underbogie needs near-every-frame because a part passing underneath doesn't get a second look.
  5. Every frame is stamped with a full identity envelope before it goes anywhere else — camera, index, timestamp, session. This is what lets synchronization later be a lookup, not a guess.
  6. Ledgers record what actually happened, not what was expected — declared frame count vs. actual extracted count, with gap ranges explicit. This is the raw material the coach-assignment step in §3 depends on.
  7. The barrier only waits on completeness, not on cross-camera agreement — genlock already guarantees agreement; the barrier just confirms all 7 ledgers exist before declaring the pass ready to process.

02Preprocessing

Three stages apply to every frame regardless of source, then each camera role gets its own specialized chain. Two things are easy to skip in a first build — geometric calibration and low-light handling — and both are exactly what the MVIS spec's measurement-based defects (buffer height in mm, wheel-shelling depth in mm) depend on.

Crit dashed box = a physics limit, not an engineering gap. Warn = flagged but non-blocking. Trunk above the fork is the only preprocessing shared by all 7 cameras.

Walkthrough

  1. Quality gate runs before anything expensive. Blur, brightness, occlusion — reject here, and every downstream stage (undistort, tile, model inference) never has to touch a useless frame.
  2. Undistortion is not optional. Every camera has lens distortion. Skip correction and every pixel-based measurement downstream — buffer height, wheel-shelling depth — is measuring a warped image, not the real part. This has to run before geometry means anything.
  3. Photometric normalization gets 7 cameras onto one distribution. Different mounts, different ambient light — models trained once need consistent input, not 7 different lighting conditions arriving unannounced.
  4. The fork happens only after the shared corrections — each role gets exactly what it needs and nothing else: OCR gets text-specific enhancement, Component gets zone-cropped before tiling (correcting each camera individually is what makes the tile trustworthy — tile first and misalignment is baked in permanently), Underbogie gets low-light handling that's always on, not conditional, because the pit is never daylight.
  5. The seam-consistency check doesn't fix anything — it flags. A lighting mismatch between two tiles usually means a dirty lens or a drifting mount, and that's a maintenance signal worth surfacing, not silently averaging away.
  6. Wheel-shelling depth is called out as a hardware limit, not a software gap. A single 2D RGB camera cannot reliably resolve sub-2mm surface depth. No amount of preprocessing changes that — this defect class needs a different sensor (laser line scanner or structured light), or it should be marked out-of-scope for the current rig rather than quietly under-detected.

03Coach assignment & synchronization

This is the corrected mechanism. The OCR camera does two jobs at once: it reads the plate number, and it watches for the physical gap between two coaches — the brief moment where no coach body is in frame. Every gap gets a timestamp. Everything captured between two consecutive gap timestamps, from any of the 7 cameras, belongs to whichever coach number was read in that same window.

Warn dashed = the two places this design admits it could be wrong (genlock drift, camera gaps) and handles explicitly. Crit = a mismatch worth stopping for, not silently absorbing.

Walkthrough

  1. The OCR camera is the boundary detector, not just the reader. It watches continuously across the whole pass. When no coach body or plate is visible for a short run of frames, that's a gap — the physical space between two coaches — and it gets a timestamp the moment it's detected.
  2. Two consecutive gaps define one coach's window. Everything the OCR camera reads between gap[i] and gap[i+1] belongs to one coach. The first coach's window starts at session start (no preceding gap exists), and the last coach's window ends at session end.
  3. The coach number itself comes from a vote, not a single read. Multiple frames fall inside each window; take the highest-confidence or majority OCR read across all of them as that window's coach_id, rather than trusting any single frame.
  4. Sanity-check against the known consist. If the train's expected coach count is known (from a manifest or consist list), compare it to the number of windows detected. A mismatch means a gap was missed (two coaches merged into one window) or falsely triggered (glare, reflection) — flag it for a human to look at rather than silently mis-assigning components to the wrong coach.
  5. Genlock does the rest of the work for free. Once a coach's window is defined in the OCR camera's frame-index space, the identical index range applies directly to all 6 other cameras — no separate boundary detection needed per camera, because they share the same clock by construction.
  6. Coverage gaps are still checked per camera, per window — genlock guarantees timing alignment, not that every camera actually captured every frame. A dropped frame on one Component camera during a coach's window still needs its own completeness check.
  7. The Coach Envelope is the one object everything downstream reads. It's built once, deterministically, before any model runs — coach_id, frame range, per-camera completeness, calibration profile — so there's no live join, no timeout, and a clean audit record of exactly what was captured for that coach.

04Dashboard data hierarchy

The Coach Envelope is the engineering object. What a station operator actually looks at is simpler: Train number → Coach number → Components (Undercarriage + Bottom). This is that hierarchy, built directly from the coach-assignment mechanism above and the defect taxonomy already established.

⇄ marks the ~7 cross-domain defect classes — they show up under both buckets because they genuinely need both camera views. Not a data-entry mistake, an honest reflection of the taxonomy.

Walkthrough

  1. Train number is the root — read once from the OCR camera, shared by every coach in the pass.
  2. Coach number comes straight from the gap-window mechanism in §3 — each coach the operator sees on the dashboard is exactly one Coach Envelope, no separate lookup needed.
  3. Two buckets under each coach, matching the two things an inspector actually walks under and around — Undercarriage (what the 2 underbogie cameras saw) and Bottom/Component (what the 4 tiled component cameras saw). This mirrors the physical inspection mental model, not the internal model architecture.
  4. Cross-domain defects appear in both buckets, marked, not hidden. An operator searching "brake" under Undercarriage should find it — and the same entry under Bottom should say so plainly, rather than picking one bucket and losing the other half of the evidence trail.
  5. Leaf level is the actual actionable unit — a defect entry with a thumbnail, a confidence score, and a confirm/dismiss action, not a raw model output.
